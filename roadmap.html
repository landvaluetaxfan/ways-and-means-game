III The diagnosis

There are two games and they do not touch.

A — the narrative

  • events, choices, flags
  • prose and consequence
  • all 21 events live here
↔two thin wires

B — the parliament

  • whips, slots, divisions
  • instruments, appointments
  • nearly all the engineering
A player can finish Game A without meaningfully playing Game B. That is more fundamental than pacing, and it is why the loop feels thin when both halves are good.

The fix is one rule: every chapter puts commitments on the docket with a date, and the session’s order-paper time is the entire capacity to meet them. The player will not meet all of them. Which one they drop is the game.

VI What to cut, and what not to

  • Not consumables. The first draft of this list said cut it. That was wrong: §7.2 is LOCKED and closure is the sovereignty number. A dead number is more often unwired than unwanted — check the wiring before reaching for the knife.
  • The constituency roll as a player-facing system. Keep the arithmetic, drop the ambition of coverage.
  • Foreign affairs as a map. Light-lag as a chart is the version that never gets finished. Ship the price.
  • Opposition mode. §3.6 is LEANING and it is a second game. Decide against it properly rather than leaving it open for years.
