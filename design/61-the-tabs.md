# 61 — The tabs, overhauled

**29 September 2026.** Decided in the first round of answers the same
day, except for Orbit, which is still open (see below). The author:
"while I like chamber, it definitely still is really packed, and other tabs
are under-designed." design/58 keeps every tab but the Record, and puts the
brief first. This record examines each tab as it stands and proposes what it
becomes. The examination used screenshots of the opening sitting at 1366 ×
768, so a tab that fills up later in a run looks emptier here than it will.

Three rules from design/58 run through every tab:
- **Readouts in words, with the figures on hover.**
- **Levers are open, matter first.** Each matter in the brief shows its own
  levers, so a tab is where the player reads and acts on their own reading,
  not where they hunt.
- **Decided: a lever that is not open is not listed.** It appears when it
  opens, and the brief says so.

## Sitting

**As it stands:**
- the Wire, a third of the width, empty at the opening;
- the page or the decision in the centre;
- on the right: Today, the docket, the calendar and the indicators, which
  are bars with figures.

**Decided:**
- **Left: what has happened.** The Wire and the Record become one column:
  news as it lands and the decisions taken, sitting by sitting. The
  Concordance's history carries the same record secondarily.
- **Centre:** the page and the decision, as now.
- **Right: the brief.** Up to four matters replace Today and the docket.
  Each matter is:
  - the minister's note;
  - its figures;
  - its remedies, each with its lever and how long it takes.

  The calendar stays under the brief, and the indicators become readouts
  in words.
- **The playtest transcript** moves out of play to the Options menu.

## Government

**As it stands**, four unrelated columns:
- **Instruments:** about twenty statutory instruments, most of them greyed
  out;
- **Document:** a reader for one order;
- **What the government can do:** six initiatives and nine ladder levers
  marked "not open to you";
- **Cabinet:** appointment cards.

It is packed with things the player cannot use yet, and its people are
buried in the last column.

**Decided: Government is organised by department.** There is one card for
each post in the cabinet, in order of seniority. Each card holds:
- the minister, with their reputation in a word (design/58: sound, uneven,
  green) and how they stand with the Prime Minister;
- the matters they have raised;
- their forecasts, and whether each came true;
- the instruments and initiatives the department can make or start. For
  example, the Treasury holds Treasury bills, exchange control, the
  approach to Earth's banks and the ladder as it opens; Life Support holds
  the licensing orders.

A vacant post shows as a card with nobody on it and nothing raised, which
is design/58's rule made visible. The document reader becomes an overlay
opened from an instrument. The undertakings, the Tribunal and the
Presidency sit in a narrow column beside the departments.

This is the tab the advice lives in, so it is the one that most needs the
overhaul.

## Chamber

**As it stands**, eight panels:
- order-paper time;
- the order paper;
- the selected bill, cut off at the foot of its column;
- the Parliament diagram, with a party legend and the division forecast;
- the whip;
- outside the chamber;
- confidence;
- composition;
- the functional constituencies.

**Two of these duplicate others:**
- order-paper time lists the same eleven bills as the order paper, only to
  carry the Grant buttons;
- the diagram's legend lists the same parties and seats as the
  composition table.

Confidence is a single line that the status bar already shows.

**Decided: the same look, with the duplicates merged.**
- The Grant button moves into the order paper's own rows, so the two
  panels become one. The time bar sits in its heading.
- The diagram's legend folds into composition.
- Confidence joins the diagram's heading.
- The functional constituencies become a drawer under composition.
- **The bill** gets the room freed on the left, so it is no longer cut
  off.
- **The whip** shows design/58's narrowing range: the Chief Whip's count
  as a range, ending as an estimate that can be off by a few.

## Economy

**As it stands**, six panels, full and mostly figures:
- the account;
- the four prices;
- the Bank and the dollar;
- the Underwriters' briefing;
- the reserve chart;
- work and trade.

**Decided: the words come first, and the big calls stand out.**
- The Underwriters' briefing, which is already prose, moves to the top as
  the reading of the account.
- Beside it goes a short list of the calls open to the player now: a tax,
  a loan, a sale, the budget line. Each call links to the matter that
  raised it.
- The Treasurer runs the routine (design/58), so the account's Draw
  buttons leave the account and become calls.
- The prices, the Bank and work stay, as readouts in words with the
  figures on hover.

## Party

**As it stands**, three panels and most of the screen empty:
- the four currents in a table;
- the selected current;
- the leadership.

**Decided: each current is treated like a partner, and the party faces
the country.**
- **Each current gets the terms Relations gives a partner:**
  - who leads it;
  - what it wants: its measures and asks;
  - what you have promised it;
  - where it parts from you;
  - how it would vote on each bill before the House.

  This is the whip's side of the price of a vote, the same as the
  partner's side in Relations.
- **The leadership:** loyalty, the paper and who is collecting names
  (Czarnecki, in Flash I).
- **The country:** the polls by band and the seats the party would win or
  lose if the count were today. The count is now the finale (design/58),
  so the player needs somewhere to watch it come.

## Relations

**As it stands:**
- the arrangement;
- the selected partner's terms;
- who votes with whom.

It is clear, with the lower half empty.

**Decided: add the Opposition.** This follows from design/58, Round F:
the Leader of the Opposition is a rival who acts. A panel for Watkins and
his party:
- what he is doing: motions, questions, approaches to your partners;
- what he is going for;
- his shadow cabinet.

His moves also reach the brief as matters.

## Orbit

**As it stands**, the stations are an electoral map:
- the stations list;
- the members for the selected station;
- the habitat schematic;
- the station's details.

The heat, which is one of Flash I's major theatres, shows only as closure.

**Open.** The author: "I actually quite like how orbit is currently. We
can discuss it more." What was proposed: **Orbit becomes the country, with
map modes.** The schematic
gets the modes the Parliament diagram already has (by party, by vote).
Here they would be:
- **by seat:** who holds it, as now;
- **by standing:** the polls;
- **by heat:** the thermal margin and consumables;
- **by order:** which orders in force touch the station.

The selected station shows its state in words. The Works appears here
when it is stranded.

## Foreign Affairs

**As it stands**, at the opening:
- the globe takes half the screen;
- "What is selected" and "Relevant actors" are empty;
- the General Assembly has nothing before it.

**Decided: what is in flight comes first.**
- **Dispatches:** what has been sent, to whom, and when an answer is due.
  Earth answers in days and Mars in weeks.
- **The actors:** always listed, each with its disposition and its delay,
  not only when one is before the government.
- **Summits ahead**, the set pieces of design/58.
- **The forums**, as now.
- **The globe** shrinks to a panel and stays the way to select an anchor.

## Concordance

It stays as it is. It carries the record secondarily.

## Answered

The author, 29 Sep, in the first round:
- Government by department;
- Chamber with its repeats merged, keeping its look;
- Party with currents as partners, and the country;
- Sitting, Economy and Foreign Affairs as proposed.

The Opposition panel and the rule on levers not yet open went in without
