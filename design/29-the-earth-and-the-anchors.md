## 2. The geography: where each anchor stands

**LOCKED BY THE AUTHOR.** The Anselm Ring elevator is **The Beanstalk**, and
the Anchorage tether is the **International Earth-Orbit Elevator**. The other
elevators in the dozen are not all Commonwealth tethers: they lead somewhere
else — the GEO free ports, the belt cyclers, and the orbital works of other
Earth states — which is what keeps the count at twelve without inventing nine
more habitats.

### The Commonwealth's four, and the names proposed for the last two

The naming has a range, and the two the author has fixed sit at opposite ends
of it: an *institutional* name (International Earth-Orbit Elevator, which is a
treaty instrument with a letterhead) and a *vernacular* one (The Beanstalk,
which is what the people who use it call it). The other two want one of each,
and the pairings below are the suggestion.

| tether | station | institutional name | the name people use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tether 1** | Anselm Ring | *(the concession's)* | **The Beanstalk** — locked |
| **Tether 2** | Anchorage | **International Earth-Orbit Elevator** — locked | *"the International"*, *"the Malindi line"* |
| **Tether 5** | Meridian Spindle | **The Meridian Vertical** | **The Clothesline** |
| **Tether 9** | Bondsville | **Bondsville Anchor No. 9** | **The Bond** |

**Why The Clothesline, and not another myth.** The Beanstalk is a myth and the
Meridian is a spindle: pairing the two as **The Beanstalk** and **The
Clothesline** sets the grand claim against the domestic object, which is the
politics of the two stations exactly. Meridian's grievance is that it has
*traffic rights it does not own*; the people who work a tether that somebody
else collects rent on do not call it by the rentier's name. The Clothesline is
a name with a grievance in it.

**Why The Bond, and why that is not a joke.** Bondsville's tether is *leased*,
so the one thing the station cannot forget about it is the instrument. **The
Bond** carries both senses at once — the station's name and the legal paper the
Commonwealth signs every generation — and it is the least sentimental name of
the four, which suits a station that lives on consumables subsidy and knows
exactly who pays.

**Alternatives if the author wants them.** Meridian: *The Spine*, *Jacob's
Ladder*, *The Long Wire*. Bondsville: *The Christmas Line* (after the anchor
site), *The Nine*, *The Coupon*.

A tether's base is not a free choice. It wants **latitude near zero** (the
counterweight hangs at geostationary, so the base must be equatorial), a
**stable host with a coastline or a high plateau** for the climber corridor,
and a state willing to grant a concession measured in generations. That is a
short list of real places, and it is politically loaded in a way that is
perfect for this game: **the best equatorial sites on Earth mostly belong to
states that the orbital Commonwealth grew rich past.**

The recommendation is to anchor the map on the real equatorial belt, which
gives the Earth antagonist a grievance that is legitimate rather than
melodramatic.

| # | tether | station served | proposed anchor site (real geography) | host |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Tether 1** | Anselm Ring | **Macapá, Brazil** — Amazon delta, on the equator, deep-water river port, and the site of a real launch range next door | Brazil / a Brazilian federal concession |
| 2 | **Tether 2** | Anchorage | **Malindi, Kenya** — 3° south, Indian Ocean, and the site of an existing space centre | Kenya |
| 3 | **Tether 3** | (a ring station of the author's choice) | **Pontianak, Indonesia** — within a kilometre of the equator, on Borneo's coast | Indonesia |
| 4 | **Tether 4** | The Bourse | **Kourou, French Guiana** — 5° north, the established equatorial launch site, EU territory | the European Union (a *European* anchor, which matters: the bloc that sanctions is not only a foreign power, it is a landlord) |
| 5 | **Tether 5** | Meridian Spindle | **São Tomé, Gulf of Guinea** — island, 0.3° north, and a state whose entire revenue is the concession | São Tomé and Príncipe (the lease state) |
| 6 | **Tether 6** | Halvard Works | **Leticia, Colombia** — 4° south, Amazon tri-border, a frontier the state barely administers | Colombia |
| 7 | **Tether 7** | Layover | **Kismayo, Somalia** — 0.4° south, Indian Ocean, in a region that has been contested for a century | a Somali federal authority |
| 8 | **Tether 8** | Rookworks-Anselm | **Port-Gentil, Gabon** — 0.7° south, the oil coast, a state that has run on extraction before | Gabon |

| 10 | **Tether 10** | (author's choice) | **Entebbe, Uganda** — on the equator, on Lake Victoria. The one **inland** anchor, and therefore the one whose corridor crosses somebody else's airspace | Uganda and its neighbours |
| 11 | **Tether 11** | (author's choice) | **Ecuador, Chimborazo/Esmeraldas** — the classic equatorial site, and a state with a history of being paid badly for what it sells | Ecuador |
| 12 | **Tether 12** | (author's choice) | **Male, Maldives** — 4° north, a low-lying island state whose tether base is also the highest ground it owns | the Maldives |

Three things this map buys, and they are the argument for it:

1. **The Commonwealth is the rich tenant, not the underdog.** Every anchor
   sits on soil whose state gets a concession fee and a berth of jobs, while
   the value flows up the tether. Tether 4 on EU soil and Tether 5 on a
   microstate's are the two ends of the same relation.
2. **The bloc and the host are different actors, and their interests
   diverge.** The EU can sanction *because* it has property at risk; Kenya
   cannot, *because* it needs the fee. That is a real diplomatic problem and
   no one has to be a villain for it to bite.
3. **The transit subsidy clause becomes legible.** "The anchor states, where
   the tether is the only way in" is now a list of real places with real
   economies, and the House is voting on their margins.

**Naming note.** Numbers alone are cold. If the author wants names, the
suggestion is to let **stations name their own tether** — Tether 2 is *the
Malindi line* to the people who work it, and the two names in circulation are
the politics: the concessionaire's number, the workers' place.

## 3. The antagonist Earth states: four options

The data has carried **two** Earth actors since Flash I, and the distinction is
worth keeping: `earth_bloc`, which can sanction, and `earth_host`, whose soil
the platform's anchor stands on. They should **not be the same state** — the
whole crisis is better if the state with the grievance and the state with the
legal order are different states that do not agree.

Four options for the pair, none of which requires a villain:

**Option A — the bloc as landlord, the host as neighbour (recommended).**
The bloc is the **European Union**, which holds Tether 4 on its own territory,
has the financial plumbing to freeze accounts, and enforces a labour and
personhood law the Commonwealth's economy deliberately evades. Its grievance is
legitimate: the orbital franchises are undercutting European workers and
European courts cannot reach them. The host is **Kenya**, whose Tether 2 the
abandoned platform was served by: a middle power with a real bureaucratic
state, a serious space programme, and a procurement law written to stop
exactly what the corporation now wants. Both are doing their jobs. The game
is that their jobs are incompatible.

**Option B — the bloc as metropole, the host as the abandoned periphery.**
The bloc is a **North Atlantic treaty organisation of the old powers**, whose
banks hold the paper and whose law the Commonwealth was founded to escape. The
host is a **smaller Gulf or African state** that signed a 99-year concession
in a bad decade and has watched the traffic grow for sixty years. The
renegotiation is a decolonisation, and the Commonwealth is on the wrong side
of it. Sympathetic to the host, cold-eyed about the bloc.

**Option C — the host as the true antagonist, and sympathetic anyway.**
The host state is a **state whose corporate champion the platform was**, and
which is quietly refusing to rescue the workers because the rescue would set a
precedent for its own labour law. Its repatriation plan is two years because
two years is the point. This is the hardest version and the best written one:
the antagonist is a government protecting its own institutional order with
other people's lives, which is what governments do.

**Option D — the competitor.** Instead of a hostile bloc, the antagonist is a
**Brazil or Indonesia** with its own tether, its own orbital programme, and a
reasonable ambition: it wants the Commonwealth as a customer paying market
rates, not as a dependent. It is not against the Commonwealth; it is against
the *terms*. This option has the most legs for a longer arc, because it can
become an ally.

**What to avoid, and why.** A bloc that simply hates the Commonwealth, or a
host that laughs at the stranded workers, makes the crisis a morality play and
the player's choice easy. The measured version is that every state in the
dispute can state its case in one sentence that the player cannot refute.

## 4. The corporation: Cordell

> **LOCKED 18 SEPTEMBER 2026 — the author has taken Cordell, chartered in
> Gabon.** The longer slate that preceded this decision is kept at the end of
> the section, because the reasoning is worth having and a name is the easiest
> thing in this document to change late.

### 4.1 The names, and which is which

A corporation of this age has four names and uses all of them, and a player
learns who is speaking from which one is on the page.

| where | the name |
|---|---|
| **The charter** | **The Cordell Extraction Company** — the name on the Act of the Gabonese Assembly that created it in 2044, and the name in every contract it has ever signed |
| **The listed parent** | **Cordell Group S.A.** — the *société anonyme* on the exchange, in the annual report, and in the sentence "Cordell Group S.A. confirms that Ashen Reach Operations was a separate legal person." |
| **Business as** | **Cordell** — one word, on the hull plates, the pay slips and the platform canteens. The company uses the short form wherever it wants to be liked. |
| **What the workers call it** | **Cordell** too, and the tone is the whole difference. This is the company town's name for the company, and it is not affectionate. |
| **What the benches call it** | **"the Cordell crowd"**, in the House; **"the Consortium"**, in the *Spindle*, which is a century out of date and knows it |

**Cordell** is the founder's name and there is no founder left to soften it. The
company has outlived the man by two hundred years, which is what makes it a
corporation rather than a business: the family sold out in the 2080s, the
charter state bought in during the 2110s when it wanted a national champion, and
what remains is a name, a charter and eleven platforms.

### 4.2 A successor, and to what

The author's question was whether the corporation should be the **direct
successor of a prominent present-day company**. It is — in structure and
history, and not in trademark.

**It is the successor to the charter houses, not to a single firm.** Its own
origin story, which it tells in the first paragraph of the annual report, is
that it was chartered in 2044 to do what the chartered extraction companies had
always done: **open ground nobody else would, under a state's protection, and
keep the leases when the state changed.** It descends from the East Africa and
Rio Tinto pattern, from the charter companies that ran territories, and from the
sovereign-linked miners that replaced them — not from one of them.

That lineage does three things for the story, and they are why I would keep it:

1. **The player understands it instantly.** A chartered extraction company with
   a national fund on the register and a history of operating where refusal was
   expensive needs no explanation. The recognition is the point; the name is not
   the point.
2. **It makes the 2287 grievance continuous with now.** The extraction logic did
   not change between the twenty-first century and the twenty-third. Only the
   property did. Ashen Reach is a depleted mine and a workforce that has become
   the liability, and that is a story the present already has.
3. **It gives the corporation its own defence, and it is a good one.** *"We were
   chartered in 2044 and we have kept every term we signed. The Commonwealth has
   not."* The company is not lying. That sentence is true, and it is the reason
   the ring-fenced wind-up is the whole villainy: **it did not break the law, it
   used it**, which is what a corporation is for.

### 4.3 The shape

- **Charter and home.** **Chartered in Gabon in 2044**, incorporated as a
  *société anonyme*, listed in London and Singapore, and majority-held by the
  **Gabonese sovereign fund** plus a pension consortium. Gabon has run on
  extraction for two centuries and it is the one country in the dispute with a
  national champion rather than a grievance — which is the more interesting
  politics, and which makes the Commonwealth the imperial tenant rather than
  the underdog. Its competitors call it a national champion. It calls itself a
  resource company.
- **Business.** Substrate feedstock, regolith metals and thermal management —
  the three things a habitat cannot make for itself quickly. It operates
  **eleven extraction platforms** and holds **two anchor concessions**, of which
  the Port-Gentil line at Tether 8 is the oldest.
- **The platform.** *Ashen Reach*, a processing platform of 300,000 workers over
  a depleted feedstock body, served by the **International Earth-Orbit
  Elevator** and leased from Kenya on a fifty-year term. It is not a failure. It
  is a legacy asset whose ore grade has fallen for a decade and whose workforce
  is the most senior, most unionised and most expensive in the group.
- **The abandonment, and why it is not villainy.** The platform is operated
  through a **special-purpose subsidiary**, *Ashen Reach Operations*, whose
  liabilities are ring-fenced from the parent. When the substrate price moved and
  the ore grade fell, the board's advisers produced two plans: a
  **decommissioning** (eleven years, and the workers are carried) and a
  **solvent wind-up** (the subsidiary is liquidated, the workers' claims rank as
  unsecured creditors, and the parent's exposure ends). The board took the
  second and took it legally. This is the single most important sentence in the
  corporation's characterisation: **it did not break the law, it used it**,
  which is what a corporation is for.
- **The debt, and who holds it.** The wind-up left bonds outstanding, secured on
  the feedstock leases and on the **instance and substrate collateral of the
  workforce** — the rents people pay to keep running. **Circumterrestrial
  Underwriters** hold the senior tranche and have already repriced the
  Commonwealth's own premium; the Earth banks hold the rest. That is why the
  crisis reaches the Commonwealth's solvency without a shot being fired.
- **The board, and its politics.** A chair from the sovereign fund, a chief
  executive who came up through the Port-Gentil anchor, and a general counsel
  who has never lost a case by answering a question directly. They are not
  cackling. They believe the wind-up was the correct allocation of capital,
  and they can defend it at a hearing without saying anything untrue.

### 4.4 The names that were not taken

Kept because a name is the easiest thing here to change and the reasoning is not.
Fourteen options in the register the setting's other corporations use — *[name] +
[sector noun]*, as in *Bellweather Consumables*, *Anselm Elevator*, *Standard
Substrate* — grouped by how real extraction companies name themselves:

**Founder-named** (coldest when the founder is long dead): **Cordell Extraction**
(taken), *Vandeleur & Sons*, *The Bellamy Concern*, *Bergstrom Group*.
**Place-named** (a company that took the name of what it dug): *Ashen Reach
Holdings*, *Port-Gentil Extractive*, *Ostrava Mineral*.
**Claim-named** (a boast, and in this game a lie): *Aurelia Resources*, *Terra
Nova Extraction*, *Halcyon Extraction Group* (the working name), *Prima Materia*.
**Legal-form** (a *Konzern*, a trust — European and slightly archaic): *The
Ostrava Concern*, *The Bergstrom Trust*, *Cordell & Reyes, Charterers*.

- **The levers it holds.** It is a **metanational**, so it belongs in
  `st.foreign` and not `st.parties`: it cannot be whipped, and it buys
  functional seats at home (the **Anchor Party** and the consortium Liberals
  already exist in the bible as its political expression). It lobbies the
  chamber in both directions, and its asks are already written: *the anchor
  concessions renewed without ratification, on their terms.*
- **What it wants, stated fairly.** The leases honoured, the debt recognised,
  and the precedent of a state rescuing its workers' claims not set — because
  if the Commonwealth assumes the bonds, every extraction charter on Earth is
  repriced.

**Where it goes in content.** It is the `earth_host` corporation in the crisis
chain, so it becomes the named counterparty in `f1_stranded`, `f1_dilemma`,
`f1_loan` (the mining-lease condition is its paper) and the substrate debt
position built in the economy pass. It is also a standing actor on the foreign
panel, with a lag, an ask, and a standing that moves.

## 5. Mars: ten names

Eleven sittings away, and the whole point of the fourth row on the foreign
panel is that its news is a fortnight old. Real Martian geography is the
right source: Tharsis, Hellas, Valles Marineris, Utopia, Elysium, Noachis,
Syrtis Major, Chryse.

1. **The Tharsis Compact** — the mining charters' treaty; the resource bloc,
   run by the companies, contemptuous of Earth and dependent on it.
2. **The Hellas Assembly** — the oldest settlement, in the deepest basin; the
   scientific and civil polity, the one that thinks it is the real Mars.
3. **The Valles Authority** — the equatorial canal cities; trade, water and
   the port politics of a trench a continent long.
4. **The Utopia League** — the northern ice settlements; water as a
   commodity and a commons, and the most exposed to Earth's prices.
5. **The Elysium Concordat** — a treaty of the eastern colonies; small,
   federal, and the one most likely to be talking to the Commonwealth.
6. **The Noachis Mandate** — an Earth-chartered mandate under the old
   settlement, still nominally administered from Earth, which nobody on Mars
   has believed in for fifty years.
7. **The Syrtis Combine** — a corporate merger of colonies that became a
   polity by accident, and governs like a board.
8. **The Ares Federation** — the independence movement, federalist in
   structure and republican in temper, the one that answers its own mail.
9. **The Olympus Directorate** — a technocratic administration around the
   caldera; competent, closed, and the hardest to read from eleven sittings.
10. **The Chryse Basin Republic** — a breakaway that Earth does not recognise
    and the Commonwealth has not decided about, which is the opening the
    annexation story needs.

**Recommendation:** the working name **"The Martian Concord"** in
`content/actors.js` is fine as a placeholder, but *Concord* implies agreement
between equals, which is exactly what a polity eleven sittings away cannot
have. **The Tharsis Compact** (if the player's counterpart is a company
charter) or **the Ares Federation** (if it is a government) each say more in
one word. What Mars wants is also the sharpest line available: it wants the
Commonwealth's **stated position on the metanationals**, because a Martian
polity is a customer of the same extraction companies that abandoned Ashen
Reach, and it has been watching.

### The Chryse Basin: a compound name

The author likes **the Chryse Basin Republic** and wants a second geographic
half added, the way real states are compounded (Bosnia *and* Herzegovina,
Antigua *and* Barbuda, Trinidad *and* Tobago). The pattern is nearly always
that the second name is either the older settlement or the second half of a
union that was not quite voluntary, so the second name should be a real
Martian region adjacent to Chryse Planitia, and it should carry a different
interest from the basin itself. Five options, all real geography:

1. **Chryse Basin and Ares Vallis Republic** — the basin and the outflow
   channel that feeds it. Ares Vallis is where the first successful landing
   was made, so the second half of the name is the founding myth, which is
   exactly how compound names work. The two halves agree, and that is the
   least interesting version of the tension.
2. **Chryse Basin and Acidalia Republic** — the basin and the great northern
   plain. Two plains, two economies: the settled basin and the newer ice camps
   to the north. The tension is generational, and the north is where the water
   is.
3. **Chryse Basin and Margaritifer Republic** — Margaritifer Terra, the
   pearl-bearing terra to the west, and a name a settler culture would have
   chosen on purpose. A romantic second half in a state named by surveyors.
4. **Chryse Basin and Xanthe Republic** — Xanthe Terra, the golden land: one
   half mines, one half farms, and the name has the settlement myth of a
   promised land in it.
5. **Chryse Basin and Nili Republic** — **Nili Fossae is the best**
   (recommended). It holds Mars' strongest evidence of ancient life, so the
   second half of the name is a scientific reserve and a protected site, and
   the polity is permanently divided between what the basin wants to dig and
   what Nili will not let it touch. In a game whose subject is *where the line
   between a person and property sits*, a constituent half named for the place
   where life may once have existed is the whole theme inside a single word.

**Recommendation: Chryse Basin and Nili Republic.** It gives the polity an
internal argument before it has said a word to the Commonwealth, it explains
what Mars wants from the metanationals (a customer that will not buy what comes
out of a protected site), and it means the antagonist's position is a genuine
division at home rather than a foreign policy.
