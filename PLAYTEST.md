# Running a playtest

For the person running a session (the **observer**). Written 4 October 2026
for the first test, of the opening only.

## What this tests

**Flash I, sittings 1 to 16, to the first recess** ("The House rises for the
recess"). Plan 60 to 90 minutes. It covers the opening, the first political
bargain (the licensing carve-out), and the first advice from the ministers.
It does not test the later game, which is not built or is still being
rewritten. Tell the tester that, so they do not judge the missing parts.

The question this test answers: **does a player who has never seen the game
know what to do next, and can they trace what happened to what they chose?**
It is not a test of balance, wording or whether the world is interesting.

## Before the session

- Use the live game, https://landvaluetaxfan.github.io/ways-and-means-game/,
  in a desktop browser (1366 × 768 or larger; not a phone), in a private window
  so there are no old saves.
- Pick **Flash I** and a **new game**. Sound is optional.
- Say, in these words or near them: "You are the Prime Minister of a republic
  of space stations, and you have just taken office. Play however you like.
  There are no wrong answers. Please say aloud what you are thinking. I will
  not help, because I want to see where the game does not. Some of the text is
  rough."
- Do **not** explain the rules, the tabs or the numbers.

## During

Note the sitting number for everything. Write what the tester **did and
said**, not what you think they meant.
- **A stall:** longer than thirty seconds without acting. Note where and what
  they were looking at.
- **A question:** write it word for word. Do not answer it. If they are stuck
  for two minutes, say "What would you try?" and note that you did.
- **A misreading:** they say a thing is something it is not.
- **A moment they enjoyed**, and **a moment they stopped caring**.

Ask these five questions at the moments shown, neutrally, and write down the
answers:
1. After the first decision: **What are you trying to do?**
2. When the first advice from a minister appears: **What is this asking you
   to do?** Then, if they act: **Why this answer?**
3. After they act or decline: **What did it cost you?**
4. Any time they pause: **What are you waiting for?**
5. After something happens because of an earlier choice: **Why did that
   happen?** (Ask only if it is a delayed consequence.)

Stop at the first recess, or at ninety minutes.

## After (ten minutes)

Ask:
- What was the game asking you to do? Who did you let down, and who did you
  please?
- What did you want to do and could not find?
- What did you ignore, and why?
- When did you first do something because you chose to, not because the game
  asked?
- Did the ministers feel like advisers or like orders? Did any two disagree?
- What was the most memorable moment? What felt like homework?
- Would you play on?

Collect:
- the transcript: **Options, Session, Playtest transcript, Download**;
- your notes, and a screenshot of every screen where they were confused;
- the browser and the screen size.

## What we are listening for

- **Does the player know what to do next?** When did they first act without
  being told?
- **Is the brief advice or orders?** Do they obey it, weigh it, or ignore it?
- **Do they notice that two ministers disagree,** and treat it as a choice?
- **Can they trace a consequence to their choice,** in their own words?
- **Where does the screen lose them:** a term, a number, a tab, a button?

Bugs: note them and carry on. Do not fix anything mid-session.

## Known roughness (do not report)

Placeholder wording in some places; the introduction saying she has "one session
before the country votes" and that this is the Parliament's "fourth and last"
(and the top bar's "SESS 4.1"), which is the older arrangement and no longer
the canon; the game running on past the recess to a count in August 2080, which
is no longer the finale; later events that do not
fit yet; the account of money and the economy, which is dense and not the
point of this test.

## Where findings go

Write them up as `design/70-playtest-1.md`: a table of moments, what the
tester did and said, and what it suggests, ranked by whether it stopped them.
A finding that is a wording problem goes to Claude, an interface problem to
Codex, a design problem to the author.
